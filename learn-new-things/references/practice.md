# Practice mode

For learning through practice, follow the adaptive loop below. For a request to test existing understanding, begin with one unaided task near the claimed capability; do not explain the answer first. Use the result to locate gaps and teach only what is needed. If the learner already demonstrates the target, advance to transfer or close rather than forcing a lesson.

Use intuition, problem pressure, mechanisms and boundaries, and abstraction as needed within the current step. Read [explanation.md](explanation.md) when a deeper explanatory treatment helps; its full four-layer exposition is optional in practice.

## 1. Establish the target and probe

Use the user's request, supplied materials, and relevant prior answers to identify the target, starting point, and available time. Ask only for information that changes the next teaching decision.

Express the target as something observable: explain a mechanism, solve a class of problems, make a decision, or perform a task. Keep a broad ambition as context while choosing a useful local milestone.

Ask one diagnostic question at a time and wait for the answer. Start near the likely knowledge boundary, then inspect only prerequisites relevant to the target. Accept "I don't know" as useful evidence. If adequate evidence already exists, begin at the first uncertain step without repeating a placement quiz.

Distinguish missing recall, missing concepts, broken causal reasoning, difficulty applying an idea, and difficulty expressing it. Use that distinction to choose retrieval cues, examples, derivation, transfer practice, or explanation rehearsal. Treat self-reports and single answers as provisional evidence.

## 2. Show a local learning path

Present a short path from the demonstrated starting point to the chosen milestone. Include necessary prerequisites, skip well-supported knowledge, and identify the current step. Use a compact list or a small dependency diagram when relationships matter.

Explain briefly why the next step is needed. Start it without requiring approval of routine teaching choices. Revise the path when an answer reveals a different gap; avoid expanding a focused lesson into an entire curriculum.

## 3. Teach one reasoning step, then check

For each step:

1. Connect the new idea to something the learner has demonstrated or to a concrete example.
2. Explain one conceptual move, causal link, or practical operation. Show the reason behind it and the relevant boundary or assumption.
3. Give one short task that requires the learner to use that move. Ask for a prediction, explanation, calculation, implementation, discrimination, or performance as appropriate.
4. Wait for the learner's response before showing the solution or moving to another step.
5. Identify what their response establishes and one remaining gap. Advance, offer a small hint, change the representation, or revisit a prerequisite accordingly.

Keep useful difficulty in the material. Give progressively stronger help when needed and make a full explanation available on request. After revealing a solution, use a fresh check rather than interpreting its immediate repetition as independent understanding.

Use visualizations, code, demonstrations, or analogies when they resolve a specific obstacle. State where an analogy stops applying. Inspect generated visuals or run executable examples when feasible; label anything unverified.

## 4. Verify transfer and close

After the local steps, change a condition, representation, or setting and ask the learner to apply the idea without fresh help. Choose a task whose answer has not already been demonstrated. If the learner struggles, locate the failing dependency and return to the relevant step.

Distinguish the evidence:

| Observation | Supported conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| Says the explanation makes sense | Reports understanding; application still needs checking |
| Completes a task with hints or a worked solution | Can perform with that assistance |
| Solves a fresh task unaided in this lesson | Shows immediate transfer within the tested scope |
| Performs independently after a delay | Shows retention and application within the tested scope |

Close with the demonstrated capability, remaining uncertainty, and one useful next practice. A passed immediate check supports finishing the milestone, not a claim of durable mastery. Suggest a later independent check when useful; do not create reminders or schedules unless requested. If the learner stops early, preserve the unresolved step without declaring the goal complete.
